## Priority corrections

1. **Wet quay:** strengthen a few narrow, broken golden reflections from the left lamps toward the bottom-left edge and from cabin windows toward the lower right. Make them follow source positions and break across stone joints; avoid spreading an amber wash over whole stone faces. This is the largest remaining visual difference.
2. **River:** darken the base water, reduce evenly distributed silver/amber flecks, and group warm highlights into elongated, irregular vertical columns beneath far-bank windows. Use mostly horizontal ripple fragments inside those columns and leave dark quiet water between them.
3. **Surface finish:** reduce high-frequency grain on the canopy, timber and plaster. Use larger restrained painted patches, dark structural seams and selective board-edge highlights. Brighten the stacked crates just enough to reveal their framing and add sparse corner fasteners.
4. **Far bank and facade:** vary distant roof heights and add a few chimneys or gables with restrained warm windows; then add the conspicuous left-wall drainpipe, slack cables and cage lantern shapes. Maintain the existing major object placement.

The improvement is real, but a passing visual match would require more than adding detail: warm reflected light and the quieter painted surface treatment still determine the scene's resemblance.
